## Stock risks and fixes

### U1 STM32G0B1CBT6: blocker, no drop-in at JLC/LCSC
Every JLC/LCSC listing of the 48-pin G0B1/G0C1 in the GP pinout shows **0 stock** in LQFP-48:
- STM32G0B1CBT6 (C2847904)
- STM32G0B1CCT6 (C5270241)
- STM32G0B1CET6 (C5270267)
- STM32G0C1CET6 (C3029185)

The **…T6N** listings (C5270160, C2978903, C3227066) also show 0, and they **would not be drop-ins anyway**. DS13560 gives separate "GP" and "N" pinouts; for example LQFP48 pin 30 is PC6 on GP parts and VSS on N parts.

Options, from least to most disruptive:
1. **Re-check on order day, or pre-order through JLC.** No ETA is given.
2. **Buy it elsewhere** (DigiKey/Mouser/ST) and consign it to JLC through its parts-consignment flow. Distributor stock and whether consignment works with *Economic* PCBA are both **UNVERIFIED**.
3. **STM32G0B1CEU6, UFQFPN-48, 512 KB** (C2829190, Extended, JLC stock 495, $5.26). Same GP pin numbering as LQFP48 (DS13560 pin table has a shared "LQFP48 / UFQFPN48 – GP" column), so firmware and the netlist stay the same. It **needs a new footprint and re-routing around U1**, and how to connect the exposed pad must be checked in DS13560. This is a layout change, T's call. (STM32G0B1CBU6, C5270156, has only 6 in stock.)
4. LQFP-64 STM32G0B1RBT6 (C5143679, 1,078 stock, $2.95). Different package and pin numbers, so it's a big change. Not recommended.

## Part upgrades for the known open issues (recommendations only; T decides)

### 1. F1: PTC rated 30 V, below the ≈ 39 V TVS clamp
I found **no 1206 resettable fuse rated ≥ 60 V at ~0.5 A hold** at JLC. The smallest 60 V/0.5 A PTCs are 1812.

| Option | Part | LCSC # | Class | JLC stock | $ @10 | Key specs | Drop-in? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A: one-time fuse, same pads** | Littelfuse **0466.500NRHF** | **C151133** | Extended | 223,487 | 0.0719 | 1206, 0.5 A, **63 V**, 50 A breaking capacity, very fast-acting, non-resettable | **Yes** (Fuse_1206 pads) |
| B: resettable, 1812 | TECHFUSE mSMD050-60V | C70113 | Extended | 217,791 | 0.0877 | 1812, I_hold 0.5 A, I_trip 1 A, **60 V**, I_max 100 A | **No.** Needs `Fuse_1812_4532Metric` (about 4.5 × 3.2 mm) and about 1.3 mm more space next to J2/D3 |
| B′: resettable, 1812, same maker as today | BHFUSE BSMD1812-050-60V | C883142 | Extended | 36,883 | 0.0889 | 1812, 0.5 A / 1 A, 60 V, I_max 40 A | No (1812) |

Context on what F1 actually has to do:
- F1 carries only the board's own supply current (tens of mA). The AUX_OUT load current returns through J2 pin 8, Q1 and GND; it never passes through F1.
- F1's real job is to open when D3 crowbars, i.e. during a reverse battery or a sustained overvoltage.
- A tripped PTC only sees the full source voltage if something downstream is shorted. So the 30 V rating matters in combined-fault cases (sustained >30 V plus a downstream short), not in normal clamping.

**Recommendation:**
- If T is touching the layout anyway (the board is being edited now), use **B (C70113, 1812 60 V PTC)**. It keeps the resettable behaviour the design intends.
- If no layout change is wanted, use **A (C151133)**. It is a true drop-in with a 63 V / 50 A rating. The cost: a reverse-polarity hookup blows it, and fixing that means replacing a 1206 part.
- Either is better than the 30 V part for a permanent install.

### 3. D4 power-LED quiescent draw
- With the datasheet Vf (2.6–3.1 V at 5 mA) and R1 = 1k from 3.3 V, the real current is about **0.2–0.8 mA**, not the README's 1.3 mA. UNVERIFIED; measure it.
- The always-hot draw is dominated by the **MCU itself (about 8.6 mA typ at 64 MHz, DS13560 Table 28)** unless firmware uses Stop modes.

**Recommendation: raise R1 to 4.7k** (UNI-ROYAL 0603WAF4701T5E, **C23162, Basic**, 15.8 M in stock, $0.0028).
- About 0.1–0.17 mA, and the 430 mcd emerald part will still be visible.
- Zero layout change and zero fee.

Alternatives:
- Mark D4 **DNP** for permanent installs (0 mA, but no power indication).
- Moving the LED to a GPIO needs a schematic change. It only helps if the MCU sleeps, and then the LED is off anyway.
