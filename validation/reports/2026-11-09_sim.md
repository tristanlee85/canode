# Step 6: Analog simulation (2026-11-09)

**Result: FAIL on load dump, CONCERN on reverse battery, PASS on the divider and LDO start-up.**
The main problem is not the 30 V PTC or the 30 V FET. It is **D3 (SMBJ24A)**: its breakdown (26.7–29.5 V)
sits below the 35 V suppressed load dump that modern cars present, so it tries to clamp that dump and
takes 16–49 J, several times what an SMB package can absorb. Raising D3's breakdown fixes that, but
it pushes the transient clamp voltage up, and then Q1, the Schottkys and the LDO need more headroom.
See [Recommendations](#recommendations).

What was simulated is **rev A as committed** (`hardware/generator/design.py` at `0b6a452`). The
working-tree schematic already has Q1 = AO3422 (55 V) and D7 = SS36 (uncommitted, made by someone
else); the 55 V-FET what-ifs below cover that change.

### Model fixes and simulator limits (stated so they can be reproduced)

- **Run end times.** Each load-dump run stops 5 ms after its pulse ends. In unsuppressed cases with no AUX load, the board browns out behind the tripped PTC: the LDO hiccups in UVLO, which is physical (the PTC needs seconds to cool and reset). After the pulse, the TI model's ideal UVLO comparator makes the timestep collapse. Recovery isn't needed for any of the metrics. `run_all.py` now **fails loudly** if any run aborts. Earlier silent truncation was caught and fixed this way.
- **USB start-up edge.** The USB start-up uses a 100 µs VBUS edge. With a 1–5 µs edge, the TI model's internal 1 µF (behind its UVLO switch) charge-shares with C1, VIN dips back under UVLO, and the comparator chatters until ngspice aborts. That is a model artifact.
- **Rejected model patch.** I tried shrinking that internal capacitor to 10 nF in a working copy. It fixed the chatter but made the model pump VIN to 75 V during brown-out, which is not physical, so I reverted it. The vendor model is used exactly as published.
- **What the model can't check.** It has no IN-pin abs-max behavior. The LDO's input exposure is therefore reported as the VIN voltage versus the datasheet limits: **40 V operating, 45 V abs max, "withstands 45 V for 200 ms"** (SLVSCJ8B, verified).

## Sim 2: load dump (Q1 off)

**Model.**
- **Generator:** 13.5 V baseline stepping to US through Ri. The "vehicle bus" node after Ri feeds J2-1 (20 mΩ + 1 µH), AUX_IN, and the external AUX_OUT load.
- **Test A** (unsuppressed): 87 V / 1 Ω, flat top for 300 ms (the plan's worst case), plus an ISO-like shape (10 ms rise, decaying to 10 % at 300 ms).

- **TVS rating comparison:** 600 W for 10/1000 µs equals 0.87 J over an equivalent 1.44 ms. For longer pulses the allowance is scaled with √t (adiabatic). That is an optimistic extrapolation of the datasheet Ppk–td curve beyond 10 ms (SMBJ steady-state dissipation is only a few watts), so exceeding it is a clear fail and staying under it is not proof of survival.

with td 300 ms, into a 35 V clamp, comes to about **120 J**. That is DO-218 load-dump-TVS territory or
an active surge stopper, not an SMB.

## Recommendations

Electrical requirements only; sourcing is being handled separately.

1. **Design target: ISO 16750-2 test B (35 V suppressed), not test A.**
   - Modern vehicles like the targets clamp load dump centrally. Test A would need about 120 J of clamping (DO-218-class load-dump TVS) and an F1 rated ≥ 100 V, or an active surge stopper / OV-disconnect with a ≥ 100 V series FET.
   - Say in the README that the board is not test-A rated.
2. **Change D3 now. This is the actual failure.**
   - **Requirement:** VBR(min) ≥ 36 V, so D3 doesn't conduct at 35 V, and standoff ≥ 26 V for jump-start (SMBJ24A sits right at its 24 V standoff during a 24 V jump).
   - **Candidate:** an SMBJ33A-class part (VBR 36.7 V min, VC 53.3 V at 11.3 A) meets this. Simulated: **0 A in test B** and only 0.01 J on pulse 2a.
   - Keep it unidirectional: the forward clamp is what handles reverse battery and negative pulses.
3. **Everything on VBATF, VIN and AUX_OUT must then handle about 54 V transients** (62 V if C1 is moved behind a series resistor) and 35 V DC for 400 ms:
   - **Q1:** VDS ≥ 60 V, preferably avalanche-rated (EAS specified). The 55 V AO3422 in the working tree is enough with SMBJ24A (42.9 V on pulse 2a), but has only 2 % margin with SMBJ33A (53.8 V). Separately, either the body diode must be rated for a ~15 A, 15 ms pulse, **or add 2.2 Ω (0805) in series with D7**, which cuts the reverse-battery current through Q1 from 14.6 A to 0.74 A.
   - **D1, D2, D7:** VRRM ≥ 60 V. D2 is already at 44 V reverse on pulse 2a as built. D7 → SS36 is already in the working tree; D2 needs the same.
   - **LDO**, either of:
     - (a) VIN abs max ≥ 60 V; or
     - (b) keep the TPS7B69 (40 V operating / 45 V abs) behind **10 Ω from D1 to VIN plus ≥ 8 µF effective at 35 V on VIN** (two 10 µF / 50 V 1210 X7R, or an electrolytic). Simulated VIN on pulse 2a: 23.6 V. Cost: 0.2–0.6 V of drop at 20–60 mA; the 4.5 V crank case still regulates.
     - In test B with SMBJ33A, VIN = 36 V, within 40 V either way.
4. **F1 to 60 V: lower priority than D3 and Q1, but cheap insurance.**
   - With D3 fixed per (2), F1 never trips from a load dump. Its worst voltage is then 13.2 V (reverse battery), so 30 V is technically enough.
   - Reasons to change it anyway:
     - A downstream short during a 35 V event puts 35 V across it.
     - Its **40 A Imax is reached in reverse battery** (39–43 A).
   - **Requirement:** Vmax ≥ 36 V minimum (60 V preferred), Imax ≥ 50 A, Ihold 0.5 A / Itrip ≈ 1 A.
5. **AUX_IN (optional):** fine for test B. To survive more, raise R13 or add a clamp at AUX_SENSE.
6. **Bottom line on "swap to 60 V parts now?"** Yes, for **Q1 and the Schottkys**, *together with* the D3 change. **On its own, swapping F1 and Q1 to 60 V while keeping SMBJ24A does not fix the real failure** (D3 overstress in the 35 V dump). Changing D3 alone, without the downstream ratings, moves the failure to Q1, D2 and the LDO on fast pulses.
